Insulation Removal

Old insulation that is contaminated, wet or flattened is not a base to build on. We vacuum it out, disinfect, correct the underlying problem and re-insulate to current code.

What it is

There is a point where adding insulation on top of what is already there is throwing money away. Insulation that is soaked, mouse-infested, smoke-damaged or compressed flat after forty years has lost most of its R-value, and burying it hides the real problem instead of fixing it — the roof leak, the bathroom fan venting into the attic, the blocked soffits, the rodent entry point.

Vacuum removal clears the space back to the deck so the deficiencies can be found and corrected, the surfaces cleaned and treated, and a fresh, full-depth insulation system installed that will actually perform.

It is also the least glamorous work we do, and the part where a careful contractor is worth the most. Old insulation is dusty, contaminated material that has to come out through your house. We contain the route, run the hose to a sealed collection unit outside, and leave the living space clean.

When removal is the right call

  • Rodent, bat or bird contamination — droppings, nesting and urine-saturated material, which is a health issue as well as an insulation one.
  • Water damage from a roof leak, ice damming or a plumbing failure — wet insulation stays wet, loses its R-value and grows mould.
  • Fire or smoke damage, where the odour lives in the insulation.
  • Decades-old material that has settled, compacted or been disturbed by repeated work in the attic.
  • Before a major upgrade, where you want a clean substrate for spray foam or a properly measured full-depth blow.
  • Vermiculite is a special case: older vermiculite may contain asbestos and must be tested before anything disturbs it. NEEDS: confirm whether you handle vermiculite, or the abatement partner you refer these jobs to

In detail

Everything else worth knowing

Old insulation? Water damage? Pest damage?

A hassle-free way of getting rid of old insulation. We remove insulation with a high-volume vacuum, and we take care of the disposal as well.

As a home gets older you can keep adding insulation, but eventually it reaches the point where the old material needs to come out instead.

Pests: mice, bats and raccoons

Over time there may have been pests in your attic — mice, bats and raccoons — that have left unwanted substances behind, and those can be quite toxic.

It is in your best interest to have that material removed once the entry points have been sealed against future pests. Removing it while the way in is still open just resets the clock.

Water damage and roof leaks

If you have ever had a roof leak, it is highly advisable to remove the water-logged insulation, because it has lost all of its R-value.

The other reason to take it out is to avoid the mould and mildew that follow trapped moisture.

Then we re-insulate to code

On completion of the removal we can re-insulate your attic to new code standards, so the job finishes with an envelope that performs rather than a hole in the ceiling.

Insulation Removal — frequently asked questions

How do I know if my insulation needs to be removed?

The usual signs are a musty or ammonia smell, visible droppings, staining on the ceiling below, ice damming, uneven heating and a heating bill that keeps climbing. If you have had a roof leak or a rodent problem, assume the insulation is affected until someone has actually looked at it.

Is the removal messy?

It is dusty work, but it should not be messy for you. The material goes out through a sealed hose to a collection unit outside, floors and the hose route are protected, and the space is cleaned when we are done.

How long does an attic removal take?

Most residential attic removals are a one-day job, with the re-insulation following immediately after or the next day. Size, access and the amount of deficiency repair drive the schedule, and we give you a realistic window in the quote — not an optimistic one.

Do I need to leave my house?

Usually not for removal. If we are following up with spray foam, that portion has its own re-entry period, which we go over with you before we start.

What about vermiculite?

Vermiculite from certain historic sources can contain asbestos, and it must be tested before it is disturbed — not vacuumed on a hunch. If you have loose, pebbly, greyish-brown insulation in your attic, do not touch it and call us to talk about testing first. NEEDS: confirm your policy — test-and-refer, or do you handle abatement?

Will you re-insulate after the removal?

Yes, and we quote it as one job. Removal without re-insulation leaves your house worse than it started, so we plan both together — including which product makes sense for each area of the attic.
