BATT Insulation

Batt insulation is only as good as its installation. We fit, cut and detail it the way the rating assumes it will be installed — which is where most batt jobs quietly lose their R-value.

What it is

Batt insulation is the product most people picture when they think of insulation: pre-cut blankets of fibreglass or mineral wool, friction-fit between studs, joists and rafters. It is the most economical way to insulate clean, standard, open framing, and for a large amount of straightforward new-build work it is exactly the right call.

What separates a good batt job from a bad one is entirely workmanship. The R-value printed on the bag assumes the batt is the correct width for the cavity, is not compressed, is not gapped at the edges, is split cleanly around wiring and plumbing rather than crushed behind it, and is paired with the right vapour barrier detailing and attic baffles. Miss those and a nominal R-22 wall can perform closer to a fraction of it — which is exactly why so many houses feel drafty despite being 'fully insulated'.

We install batts the way the rating assumes. Every cavity is measured and cut, every obstruction is split around, edges are tucked, and the barrier and baffles are detailed before anything gets covered up.

Fibreglass or mineral wool?

  • Fibreglass batts are the value choice: light, widely available, and effective in standard cavities. They are what most new-build walls and ceilings get.
  • Mineral wool (rock wool) costs more but is denser, holds its shape without fasteners, is non-combustible, and performs noticeably better acoustically — which makes it a favourite for party walls, mechanical rooms, home offices and floors between storeys.
  • We will price either, and tell you honestly where paying for mineral wool is worth it and where it is not.

Building a new home or cottage and looking for insulation? When you insulate, it matters that you choose a company offering a range of insulation styles so the right one goes in each area.

The key is tight-fitting batts

The key to a quality insulating job is tight-fitting batts that completely fill the stud cavity with no voids or gaps. Only our trained professionals install fiberglass batts the proper way, avoiding the heat-loss problems that lead to ice damming, mould and mildew.

BATT Insulation — frequently asked questions

Is batt insulation worse than spray foam?

It is different, not automatically worse. Batts do not air seal, so in a leaky house or an awkward cavity foam will outperform them by a wide margin. But in clean, standard framing that has been properly air sealed, well-installed batts hit code, cost far less and are a completely legitimate choice. The mistake is using batts where the assembly needs an air and vapour barrier.

What R-value do I need in Ontario?

It depends on the assembly, the climate zone, your heating equipment and whether it is a new build or a renovation — the Ontario Building Code sets the minimums by build type. Can I just add new batts on top of the old ones?

In an attic, sometimes — if what is there is dry, clean and uncontaminated. If it is compressed, mouse-damaged, wet or full of debris, adding more just buries the problem. We will tell you which one you are looking at, and we do vacuum removal when it needs to come out.

Do batts need a vapour barrier?

In most Ontario assemblies, yes — and the detailing matters as much as the barrier itself. Poly that is torn, unsealed at the seams or missing at the top plates is a leak path for warm, humid air, which is how condensation and mould problems start inside a wall.

Will batts help with noise between rooms?

Yes, especially mineral wool. Insulating interior partitions and the floor system is the least expensive comfort upgrade there is, and it is much easier to do during construction than after.

Do you install batts in existing homes?

Yes — anywhere the framing is open or can be opened, including basement renovations, additions, garage conversions and attic floors. If the walls are finished and staying that way, blown-in drill-and-fill is usually the better route.
