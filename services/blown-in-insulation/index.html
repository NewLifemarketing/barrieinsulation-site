Blown-In (BIBS) Insulation

BIBS blows loose fibre behind netting until the cavity is packed solid — around every wire, pipe and brace that a batt has to be cut around.

What it is

BIBS stands for Blow-In Blanket System. A tough netting is stapled across the open stud cavity, and loose fibre is blown in behind it under pressure until the cavity is completely, uniformly full. The result is a seamless blanket that flows around wiring, outlet boxes, plumbing, fire blocking and diagonal bracing instead of being cut and stuffed around them.

That is the whole point. A batt is a rectangle, and a wall cavity almost never is. Every notch, compression and gap in a batt install is a spot where the wall performs below its rating. BIBS fills what is actually there, which is why the same wall assembly measures higher when it is blown rather than stuffed.

In a 2x6 wall BIBS reaches R-24, and in a 2x4 wall R-14 — and because the fill is dense and continuous, it is also one of the best sound-control products we install.

In detail

Everything else worth knowing

The fiberglass blown-in wall system

The fiberglass blown-in wall system is the next upgrade from conventional fiberglass batt insulation. The product is injected into the wall to a manufacturer-specified density, achieving an R-24 value in a 2x6 wall cavity or R-14 in a 2x4 cavity.

Installing blown-in insulation requires a licensed contractor. The process involves applying a fiber mesh over the stud wall cavity, then injecting blown fiberglass insulation into the cavity behind it. The blown fiberglass gets in and around all wires, plumbing, electrical outlets and other hard-to-insulate areas — by far the better overall product.

The product does not settle over time, and it will not absorb moisture, so it does not lead to mould and mildew issues.

Proven to perform 50% better than batt

The blown fiberglass wall system is proven to perform 50% better than conventional fiberglass batt insulation. Installed by certified installers, you get a true working R-24 fiberglass wall with zero voids.

In most cases fiberglass batt insulation is either compressed or improperly fitted into the wall cavity, creating an enormous number of voids. That results in poor R-value and possible mould issues in your home. With the blown-in wall system you get true working insulation in a full wall cavity with zero voids.

Acoustical (soundproofing)

Even with its insulating R-value, blown-in fiberglass can be injected into interior walls and floor joist systems to give superb acoustical properties. Injected into 2x4 interior wall construction, an STC of 50 is achieved — real peace and quiet in the areas that need it.

Injected into floor joist cavities, the product gets around plumbing lines, vents and heating ducts, giving you soundproofing and insulation at the same time and stopping heating and cooling loss before it reaches its final destination.

Drill and fill — finished walls, older homes

Finished walls? An older Victorian-style home? Do you even know whether your walls have insulation in them? We can fill the wall cavities by injecting blown fiberglass insulation.

The process involves drilling 2" holes into the drywall or wood, typically from the inside of the home and only rarely from the outside.

Older homes typically had no insulation at all and relied on a double wall system with an air gap between — balloon framing, where the air gap was the insulator of the day. With today's energy costs, injecting those walls with insulation simply makes sense and puts money back in your pocket.

Blown-In (BIBS) Insulation — frequently asked questions

How is BIBS different from batt insulation?

Same family of material, completely different install. A batt is a pre-cut rectangle that has to be trimmed and fitted around whatever is in the cavity. BIBS is blown in loose behind netting and packs the cavity solid, so the small gaps and compressions that quietly cost you R-value do not exist.

Will blown-in insulation settle over time?

Not when it is installed correctly. BIBS is blown to a specified density behind netting rather than simply poured in, which is what keeps it from slumping and leaving a cold strip at the top of the wall.

Can you insulate my walls without taking the drywall off?

Usually, yes — that is what drill-and-fill is for. We drill 2" access holes, typically from inside the home and only rarely from the outside, blow the cavity full, then plug and patch. Most jobs never touch your siding or brick.

Is blown-in good for soundproofing?

It is one of the best value-for-money options. Dense fibre fill absorbs airborne sound between rooms and between floors. For a serious media room we will also talk to you about the drywall and the door, because insulation alone never solves a sound problem completely.

How much insulation should my attic have?

Modern Ontario attic targets are far deeper than what most older homes were built with, and many attics we open are at half of it after decades of settling. We measure what is up there and quote the top-up depth — and if the existing material is contaminated or wet, we recommend removal first.

BIBS or spray foam — which do I need?

If the cavity is standard, dry and accessible, BIBS gives you excellent value. If you need an air and vapour barrier, high R-value in a shallow space, or you are dealing with a rim joist, crawl space or cathedral ceiling, spray foam is the right tool. Most homes we quote end up with a combination.
