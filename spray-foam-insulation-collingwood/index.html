A town that is building, and a permit that asks about energy

Spray foam insulation Collingwood builders order today goes into a market that is moving faster than most of Ontario. The Town’s own 2023 Community Profile puts the census population at 21,793 in 2016 and 24,811 in 2021, growth of 13.85% against 5.77% for the province, and it cites the Growth Management Plan’s estimate that 6,216 housing units will be needed between 2016 and 2031. The same profile projects construction employment in Collingwood rising from 1,662 in 2023 to 2,455 by 2031, a 48% jump.

More Collingwood houses means more permits through one building department, and every one of them carries the same two energy questions. Which SB-12 path did the designer choose? And can the envelope on site prove it at inspection? The Town of Collingwood will not accept a new house application without an Energy Efficiency Design Summary, on the prescriptive or the performance method, alongside the heat loss and heat gain calculations and the mechanical ventilation form. Its stated turnaround on a complete single-dwelling application is 10 business days.

That is why this page is written for the builder, the GC and the renovator who subs insulation out, not for the homeowner. On a Collingwood new build, foam is a line on that form first, and our job is making the wall match it.

SB-12

Spray foam insulation Collingwood designers can write into the package

The path is chosen before the foam is priced; this is where foam earns a place in it.

Three paths, one form

The Ontario Association of Architects’ Practice Tip PT.36.3, updated for the 2024 Code, sets out the three routes SB-12 allows for a Part 9 house: a prescriptive compliance package under Subsection 3.1.1, a performance model under 3.1.2 that has to use less energy than a reference house built to a package, or certification under ENERGY STAR for New Homes v12.6 or R-2000 under 3.1.3. The climate zone comes first. Zone 1 is under 5,000 heating degree-days, Zone 2 is 5,000 or more, and the values are listed by location in SB-1 Table 1.2. Collingwood has its own row in that table; the Township of Clearview’s published climatic data guide says it uses that same Collingwood row.

Fuel and furnace efficiency then pick the table, and the designer picks a package inside it. Two package conditions matter to anyone pricing foam. Sentence 3.1.1.1.(14) requires rim joist areas to be insulated to the same level as the walls around them, and the window-and-door ratio decides whether the prescriptive route is open at all: above 17% the window values tighten, above 22% the house has to be modelled.

The rim joist line is a foam line

Bring the header space up to wall level in a 2x6 Collingwood house and you are filling an irregular bay, broken by joists, blocking, plumbing and wiring, that also happens to be one of the biggest air leaks in a Canadian house. Closed-cell foam at roughly R-6 per inch reaches the wall value in the depth available and seals the joints as it goes. A cut batt can reach the number on paper and leak around every edge.

The same logic holds for a cathedral ceiling with a fixed rafter depth, a floor over the garage, and a cantilever. It does not hold for a plain 2x6 wall or an open attic, and we will say so: those are usually cheaper to meet with BIBS or batt.

The airtightness credit is a whole-house test

Article 3.1.1.4 lets a builder trade one item from Table 3.1.1.4.B, or two from 3.1.1.4.C, in exchange for a tested airtightness result. The OHBA and OBOA guide to the prescriptive design summary lists the targets as 2.5 air changes per hour at 50 Pa for a detached house and 3.0 for an attached one, and PT.36.3 names ASTM E779 or CGSB 149.10 as the usual test methods.

Foam helps a house get there; it cannot get it there alone. It is an air barrier where it is sprayed, at rim joists, top plates and penetrations. Windows, doors, the attic hatch and every hole the electrician and the HVAC crew leave behind still show up on the blower door. Claiming the credit on a Collingwood house? Tell us at the quote, so the foam goes where the leaks are.

Inspection

Two inspections every spray foam Collingwood job has to clear

The Town of Collingwood lists its required inspection stages on the reviewed permit drawings. Foam lands between two of them, and the order is not optional.

  1. 1

    Framing passes first

    Collingwood calls framing ready once the roof, windows, plumbing, HVAC and electrical are in. Its insulation stage also says every framing deficiency noted must be done, so clear the list before the rig shows up.

  2. 2

    We look before we price

    A free site visit and a written quote, itemized by area and product, with the reasoning for each. On a new build, the drawings and the design summary help.

  3. 3

    Masked, ventilated, sprayed

    Windows and anything not getting foam are covered and ventilation is set before mixing. Foam goes on in controlled lifts at documented thicknesses, and other trades stay out for the re-entry period we give you up front.

  4. 4

    Air barrier and insulation inspection

    Collingwood wants the air barrier continuous and sealed across the whole envelope, usually checked at the framing or insulation visit, and the insulation and vapour barrier seen before drywall. Book it two business days ahead through the Public Portal or inspections@collingwood.ca.

  5. 5

    Then the board

    Only after a Collingwood pass does drywall go up. It is also the thermal barrier the Code requires over foam in occupied space, so plan board, or an approved coating, for the basement and garage too.

What the foam insulation Collingwood inspectors see should already tell them

  • That it was applied to the standard. Building Code Article 9.25.2.5 requires spray polyurethane to be installed to CAN/ULC-S705.2. Our applicators are certified to it, and we are a certified SPFI contractor and a member of CUFCA, the Canadian Urethane Foam Contractors Association.
  • That the thickness is documented. S705.2 work runs in controlled lifts with a daily quality record, so there is an answer when someone asks what went into a bay.
  • That the air barrier is continuous. Collingwood asks for an approved air barrier that is sealed throughout the entire envelope, so where the foam meets the rest of that system matters as much as the foam.
  • That the thermal barrier is planned. Exposed foam in a space someone will occupy needs drywall or an approved coating over it. We flag which areas need it in the quote, so nothing is a surprise at inspection.

Builders’ questions about foam on Collingwood permits

Does spray foam on a new Collingwood house need its own inspection?

It falls inside the inspections already on your permit. The Town of Collingwood lists an air barrier stage, generally done during the framing or insulation inspection, and an insulation and vapour barrier stage that has to happen before finishes and drywall. Book each one with at least two business days’ notice through the Public Portal or by email to inspections@collingwood.ca.

Which Building Code edition is my Collingwood permit under?

Ontario Regulation 163/24, the 2024 Building Code, took effect on January 1, 2025. The old regulation, O. Reg. 332/12, still governs a project permitted on or before December 31, 2024, or one whose drawings were substantially complete by then and applied for before March 31, 2025, provided construction starts within six months of the permit. SB-12 remains the energy standard for Collingwood houses under both.

Will closed-cell foam earn the SB-12 airtightness credit by itself?

No product earns it alone. The credit under Article 3.1.1.4 is claimed on a blower-door result for the whole house, tested to ASTM E779 or CGSB 149.10, and the target for a detached house is 2.5 air changes per hour at 50 Pa. Foam closes the rim joists, top plates and penetrations it is sprayed on; the windows, doors, ceiling plane and every other trade’s holes still count.

What happens if the drywall goes up before the insulation inspection?

The Town of Collingwood says anything covered before its mandatory inspection has to be uncovered and passed before the project can be completed, and that a minimum $170 fee is payable before further inspections when work was incomplete or earlier deficiencies were not corrected. Opening finished walls to show the foam is the expensive version of a two-day wait.

Does sprayed foam need drywall over it before occupancy?

In occupied space, yes: the Ontario Building Code requires foam to be separated from the interior by a thermal barrier, usually drywall, or an approved coating where drywall is impractical. On a Collingwood new build that mostly matters in unfinished basements, garages and mechanical rooms, where nobody planned board. We name the requirement for each area in the quote.
