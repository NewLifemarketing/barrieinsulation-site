Flat Roof Insulation

Keep the deck warm, or keep it dry.

November 26, 2026 · Barrie Insulation Systems

Flat roof insulation goes in one of two places, above the deck or below it, and that choice decides where the dew point sits for the life of the roof. Get it right and the deck stays warm or stays dry. Get it wrong and the deck gets wet and has no way to dry out.

Low-slope roofs on a house in Barrie turn up on additions, garages with a room over them, modern builds, shops and commercial units. The framing is usually simple. The moisture problem is not, because a membrane on top shuts off the one direction a pitched attic dries in. Here is how the three assemblies compare, where closed-cell foam under the deck earns its cost, where it causes the failure, and what has to be done before our crew shows up.

Flat roof insulation: three assemblies, one question

Every option is a different answer to one question: what keeps warm, humid indoor air off a cold deck all winter?

Flat roof insulation above the deck

Rigid board goes on top of the deck and under the membrane, often tapered polyiso to build in drainage. The deck and the joists sit on the warm side, so the joist space below can be left empty or used for services. The roofer installs it as part of the roof, not the insulator. If the roof is coming off anyway, this is the cleanest flat roof insulation assembly there is. The catch is polyiso's cold-weather R-value, which our post on polyiso foam board goes through.

Below the deck, vented

Batts or blown fibre fill the joist space, with an air space above them and vents to the outside. Subsection 9.19.1 of the Ontario Building Code requires that venting where insulation sits between the ceiling and the underside of the roof sheathing, unless it can be shown to be unnecessary. On a roof built with joists or with a slope under 1 in 6, the vent area doubles, to 1/150 of the insulated ceiling area.

A vented flat roof can work. It needs air that can actually move: open joist runs, vents on opposite sides, and nothing blocking the space above the insulation. Parapets, blocking, a stepped roofline or a long run between vents all work against it, and it depends on a perfect interior air barrier and vapour barrier at the ceiling.

Below the deck, unvented

Closed-cell spray foam goes straight onto the underside of the deck, and the vents are closed off. This is the "can be shown to be unnecessary" route, so the building official decides, and you want that conversation before the crew arrives, not at the framing inspection. Our spray foam insulation page lists roof decks among the places we spray. The foam we install, Polarfoam 7300-0 SOYA, is a 2 lb closed-cell foam at roughly R-6 per inch. The same layer is insulation, air barrier and vapour retarder at normal installed thickness, so indoor air never reaches the cold sheathing.

Where closed-cell under the deck is the right call

Foaming the deck from below is the flat roof insulation for a roof that is staying put. It makes sense when the membrane is sound and nobody is replacing it, when the joists are too shallow to fit both the R-value and a vent space, and when the joist bays are cut up by blocking, ducts and penetrations a batt has to be cut around. It is the same reasoning as a cathedral ceiling: a lot of R-value in a shallow cavity, sealed in the same pass.

It also suits the commercial and agricultural work that tends to have flat or low-slope roofs: shops, warehouses, pole buildings and outbuildings, where continuous foam over irregular framing is often what makes the space practical to heat. Foam sprayed to the deck stays where it was sprayed, with no voids for warm air to find and no batt to sag away from the sheathing.

Where flat roof insulation from below traps water

Here is the failure. A membrane on top is close to vapour-impermeable. Closed-cell foam underneath is a vapour retarder. The deck between them can dry in neither direction. That is fine for a dry deck, and that is exactly why the condition of the deck on the day we spray is the whole job.

  • A wet deck stays wet. Sheathing that took on water during framing, or from an old leak, gets sealed in with the moisture still in it. Check it with a moisture meter before anything is sprayed, and dry it out first if it reads high.
  • A leak you cannot see. Once the underside is foamed, a membrane leak no longer drips through the ceiling where someone will notice it. It runs along the deck. Fix every known leak first, and if the roof is near the end of its life, re-roof before you insulate, not after.
  • Foam too thin for the batts below it. A hybrid, foam on the deck and batts under it, works only if the foam carries enough of the total R-value to keep its inside face warm through a Barrie winter. Too little foam and the dew point lands in the batts, right at the foam face. Take the ratio from the designer or the manufacturer's guidance for the assembly, not a rule of thumb.
  • Half vented, half not. An unvented roof with the old soffit or parapet vents left open, or a vent space left over batts with foam somewhere else, is neither assembly. Pick one and detail it fully.

Open-cell foam is a different story again. It is vapour-permeable, so on its own under a cold deck it lets indoor moisture through to the sheathing. We install closed-cell, and that is one reason why. Our post on vapour barrier vs air barrier covers why the two layers do different jobs.

What has to be ready before we spray the deck

Most callbacks on flat roof insulation start before anybody mixes foam. This is the list we would want done before the crew is booked.

Roof on, dry and leak-free

Membrane complete, flashings and drains in, and the deck dry. See above. This is the one item that cannot be fixed after.

Penetrations through the deck in first

Plumbing stacks, exhaust ducts, curbs for rooftop units, conduit and any new framing or blocking. Foam seals around what is there. Anything cut through the deck afterwards punches a hole in the air barrier that someone has to come back and seal.

The unvented decision signed off

If the assembly is unvented, confirm it with the building official and with whoever is responsible for SB-12 compliance on the job. The roof R-value comes from the compliance package your designer chose. Close the vents as part of the scope, so nobody leaves them open by accident.

A thermal barrier planned

Foam in an occupied space has to be covered by a thermal barrier, usually drywall on the ceiling, or an approved coating where a ceiling is not practical, such as an open shop. Know which one before the drywaller is scheduled. Our thermal barrier post covers who owns that item, and we flag what your space needs in the quote so nothing is a surprise at inspection.

Conditions and access

Foam is installed under CAN/ULC-S705.2: controlled substrate and ambient conditions, lifts at documented thicknesses and a daily quality record. On a cold deck in January that means the space has to be within the manufacturer's application range when we spray. The work area is ventilated, the crew wears supplied-air respirators, and other trades stay out until the manufacturer's re-entry period has passed. We give you that time for your job before we start, and we schedule larger and commercial jobs around your other trades.

When the cheaper answer is the right one

Foam under the deck is not the default for flat roof insulation, and we would rather say so than sell it everywhere.

  • If the roof is being replaced, above-deck board is often the better assembly. The deck goes warm, the joist space is free for services, and that is roofing work.
  • If the venting can genuinely work, with deep joists, clear runs and vents on opposite sides, a vented joist space with batt insulation and a sealed ceiling meets code for less. Our part is detailing the batts to the Ontario Building Code without compression or gaps.
  • If the deck has a leak history and the roof is near its end, the cheapest fix is to wait. Re-roof first, then decide.

Where foam is the right tool, it is the right tool for good reasons: shallow joists, a roof staying put, framing too cut up to batt, or a building you need to heat properly. We will tell you honestly when a cheaper product will do the same job.

The decisions that matter most on a roof are cheapest to get right before the drywall goes on, so bring us in early. We walk the space, look at the deck and tell you what the assembly needs, in writing. Start with a quote request, or see where we work around Barrie.

Flat Roof Insulation — questions builders ask

What is the best flat roof insulation?

The one that suits the roof you have. On a re-roof or new build, rigid board above the deck keeps the deck warm and is usually the cleanest assembly. Under an existing sound membrane with shallow joists, closed-cell spray foam on the underside of the deck gives the most R-value per inch and seals the air in the same pass. A vented joist space with batts is the cheapest, and it only works where air can actually move through it.

Can you spray foam the underside of a flat roof?

Yes. Closed-cell foam sprayed to the underside of the deck makes an unvented assembly in which the foam is the insulation, air barrier and vapour retarder. The deck has to be dry and leak-free first, because with a membrane on top and foam below it cannot dry either way. Confirm the unvented assembly with your building official before the work is booked.

Does a flat roof need to be vented?

Under Subsection 9.19.1 of the Ontario Building Code, insulation between the ceiling and the underside of the roof sheathing needs a vented air space unless venting can be shown to be unnecessary. Low-slope and joist roofs need twice the vent area of a pitched attic. Closed-cell foam on the deck is the usual way to show venting is unnecessary, and the building official makes that call.

How much insulation does a flat roof need?

It depends on the SB-12 compliance package the designer chose for the building, which sets the R-value for a roof without an attic space. Closed-cell foam at roughly R-6 per inch reaches it in less depth than anything else we install. On a hybrid of foam and batts, the foam has to be thick enough to keep its inside face above the dew point, so take that ratio from the design, not a rule of thumb.

Do you need a vapour barrier with closed-cell foam on a flat roof?

Not a separate poly layer in most cases. At normal installed thickness, closed-cell foam acts as the vapour retarder and the air barrier. Open-cell foam does not. It is vapour-permeable and needs a vapour control layer on the warm side, which is one reason closed-cell is what we install under a deck.

Does spray foam on a flat roof need to be covered?

Yes, in occupied space. The Ontario Building Code requires spray foam to be separated from the interior by a thermal barrier, usually drywall, or an approved coating where drywall is not practical. We flag what the space needs in the quote so it is planned before inspection.
