Why insulate properly

The cheapest energy is the energy you never use

Insulation is the one upgrade that starts paying you back the day it is finished — and keeps paying every winter for the life of the building.

Up to 50%

of the energy used to heat and cool a home can be lost through an under-insulated, unsealed building envelope.

Actual savings vary with the age and condition of your home, your heating system and how you use it. We would rather look at your house than quote you a number from a brochure.

Lower energy bills

Heating and cooling is the largest line on an Ontario utility bill, and in an under-insulated house a large share of it goes straight outside. Sealing and insulating properly is the one home improvement that starts paying you back the day it is finished.

A healthier, more comfortable home

Air sealing stops the drafts, the cold floors and the room that is always five degrees off. It also cuts the moisture movement that leads to condensation, mould and ice damming.

A smaller footprint

Energy you never use is the cleanest energy there is. Less fuel burned every winter for the life of the building — and our spray foam uses soya-based renewable content in place of part of the petroleum-based resin.

Certified, code-compliant work

Spray foam installed to CAN/ULC-S705.2 by certified applicators, and every product specified to meet the Ontario Building Code requirements for your build.

Questions we get asked every week

Spray foam, blown-in or batt — how do I choose?

Match the product to the problem. Spray foam where you need an air and vapour barrier, high R-value in a shallow space, or a seal on an irregular surface — attics, rim joists, crawl spaces, cathedral ceilings. BIBS blown-in where you have standard cavities to fill completely, want sound control, or need to insulate finished walls without opening them. Batt where the framing is open, clean and standard and budget matters most. Most houses we quote get a combination, and the comparison table on our services page lays out the differences side by side.

What is R-value, exactly?

R-value measures how well a material resists heat flow — the higher the number, the slower heat passes through it. Two things get missed. First, R-value only describes conduction: it says nothing about air leaking through gaps, which in most Ontario houses is the larger problem. Second, the number on the package is the material's rating in a lab, not the wall's rating in your house. Studs conduct heat around the insulation, and every gap, compression and missed corner drops the assembly below its label. That gap between nominal and effective R-value is where good installation earns its money.

Is spray foam safe?

Cured spray foam is an inert plastic and is safe in a finished home. The precautions belong to the installation: the space is ventilated, the crew wears supplied-air respirators, and occupants stay out during application and for the manufacturer's stated re-entry period. This is exactly why the CAN/ULC-S705.2 installation standard exists and why you should only let a certified applicator spray your house.

How much will I save on my heating bill?

Enough that it is the reason most of our customers call, and not a number anyone can honestly promise you from a website. It depends on how leaky your house is right now, what you heat with, the size of the area being treated and how you run the thermostat. A poorly sealed older home with an empty attic sees a dramatic change. A ten-year-old build getting a top-up sees a modest one. We tell you which one you have after we look.
