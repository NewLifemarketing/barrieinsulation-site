Midland's permit list is a map of where foam pays

Read the Town of Midland's list of projects that need a building permit and you will find most of the places closed-cell foam does its best work: finishing your basement, adding a residential unit, enclosing a porch, putting heating or plumbing into a building. That overlap decides how to plan spray foam insulation Midland, Ontario homes need. The foam goes in after the framing and rough-ins and before the thermal barrier, so it gets booked against the permit rather than tacked on once the room is done.

Get the order wrong and the only way back to a rim joist is through new drywall.

Below: what the Town's list covers, what its fee by-law says about a bare basement, what belongs inside the finished wall, and what changes on the water.

The Town of Midland permit list

Spray foam insulation, Midland, Ontario: where it meets the permit list

The Town's Building Permits & Inspections page names these projects as permit work. Each one opens up a part of the house that foam handles better than anything else we install, for a short window.

Finishing your basement

It is on Midland's list by name. Before studs and drywall go up, the rim joist and the foundation wall are bare and easy to reach. Afterwards they sit behind a finished wall, and the cheapest fix becomes cutting it open.

A second unit in the basement

Midland allows one second unit per lot, and a basement unit may take the entire basement. Those foundation walls and that rim joist become the outside walls of somebody's home, and they deserve to be insulated like it.

Enclosing a porch, deck or patio

Any size, says the Town. The new room sits on a floor framed to have weather under it. Foam sprayed to the underside seals and insulates in one pass, which is what a floor with wind moving beneath it needs.

Heat, plumbing or a wood stove

Installing or modifying heating or plumbing, or putting in a wood burning stove, is permit work in Midland. Those are the usual signs a seasonal building is being pushed into winter, and the floor and crawl space are where it loses.

Sequence

Book the foam between the rough-ins and the drywall

Each of those Midland projects runs in roughly the same order, with one right place for foam. Plumbing, heating and wiring go in first, so nobody has to cut a line through cured foam. The foam goes on next, while every surface is still open. Then the thermal barrier covers it, and only then the finishes.

Midland's inspections fit around that. The Town processes requests entered in Cloudpermit by 11 am the same day, and inspectors are out between 1 pm and 4 pm; phone requests to 705-526-4275 ext. 2248 by 11 am get the same treatment. For a contractor running the job, the foam visit, the inspection and the drywall can sit in one tight week.

Since the start of 2025, a new Midland permit is reviewed under the 2024 Ontario Building Code. O. Reg. 163/24 builds it on the National Building Code of Canada 2020 with Ontario's own amendments, and keeps the old code only for permits issued by December 31, 2024, or for drawings substantially complete by then with the application in by March 31, 2025. If your Midland basement permit is older than that, tell us when we quote, because the code your permit was issued under is the one the work is specified to.

A bare Midland basement costs nothing to permit by area

Midland's fee by-law has a clause most homeowners never read. By-law 2026-2, passed in February 2026, says a residential basement or crawl space that is wholly below grade, with no interior finishes and no proposed use, has no permit fee applied to it. Finish it and the basement-finishing lines in the fee tables come into play, with a $134.55 minimum on low-density residential projects and $347.00 on alterations over 20 m².

Two honest readings of that.

If the basement is bare and you have no plan to finish it, sealing the rim joist and the top of the foundation wall now is a small, contained job. Midland's list does not mention insulation on its own either way, so check with Building Services at building@midland.ca before you book, and then do it while everything is open.

If you are going to finish it within a year or two, there is no prize for doing the foam twice. Put it in the finishing permit, at the step in the diagram above. The only rule is that it goes on before the wall closes.

And if the walls are already framed and you only want the stud bays filled, foam may not be the best value. Batt or BIBS fills a standard open cavity for less, and we will tell you when that is the case in your Midland basement.

Inside the wall

What a finished Midland basement wall should hold

Three layers matter in that drawing. Closed-cell foam on the rim joist, because that band of framing is one of the biggest air leaks in a Midland basement and a batt only ever bridges the gap. Foam against the foundation wall, because at roughly R-6 per inch it reaches a useful number in the shallow depth a basement wall allows, and at normal thickness it is also the vapour retarder that keeps warm room air off cold concrete. And a thermal barrier on the room side, drywall or an approved coating, because the Ontario Building Code does not let foam stay exposed in a space people use.

That last layer is why the sequence matters so much in Midland. The drywall is not a finishing touch over the insulation; it is a code requirement in its own right. When the foam and the barrier are planned together, nothing about the inspection is a surprise.

On the water

Porches, boathouses and the buildings Midland was not heating

Midland's fee by-law has its own lines for covered porches and for accessory buildings and boathouses. If one of them is getting heat for the first time, it is coming through that permit list anyway.

A building put up for summer use on piers, blocks or a vented crawl space has its floor open to wind on every side. Our four-season cottage write-up walks through why that is the first thing to fix, and why spraying the crawl space walls instead of the floor often keeps the water lines inside the warm side.

In Midland, that conversion tends to show up on the Town's list as something else: installing heating or plumbing, putting in a wood burning stove, or enclosing a porch. Any building that contains plumbing needs a permit here. So the same rule applies on the water as in a basement. Plan the foam into the job before the floor is closed in or the skirting goes back on.

Spray foam in Midland: questions people ask

Do I need a permit to spray foam my basement in Midland?

It depends on the project around the foam. The Town of Midland lists finishing your basement as permit work, along with adding a residential unit and installing or modifying heating or plumbing. Its list does not mention insulation on its own either way, so if all you want is the rim joist sealed in a bare basement, ask Building Services before booking: building@midland.ca or 705-526-4275 ext. 2248.

Can spray foam go into a basement second unit in Midland?

Yes, and it is a good place for it. Midland allows one second unit per lot in its residential and rural zones, and a unit in the basement may take the entire basement. That makes the foundation walls and rim joist the outside walls of somebody’s home. Closed-cell foam seals and insulates them in one pass. For the ceiling between the two units, where sound matters more than R-value, ask us about BIBS blown-in, which is the better sound product.

We are enclosing a porch in Midland. What should the floor get?

Enclosing a deck, porch or patio of any size needs a permit in Midland, and the floor is the part people forget. A porch floor was framed to have weather under it. Once it is a room, closed-cell foam sprayed to the underside seals and insulates it together, which is what a floor with air moving beneath it needs. We look at how it is framed and how you can get under it before we quote.

When should the foam be booked against my Midland inspections?

After the plumbing, heating and wiring rough-ins, and before drywall or any other thermal barrier goes on, so nothing is cut through cured foam or torn out to reach it. Midland inspections requested by 11 am through Cloudpermit or by phone are done the same afternoon.

Do you really come to Midland from Barrie?

Yes. We are based in Barrie and work across Simcoe County, and Midland is one of the towns we name on our own FAQ. There is no Midland office. The quote is free and done at your building, Monday to Friday, 7:30 am to 5:30 pm.

Are there rebates for spray foam in Midland?

Programs change, so we will not print a promise that expires. As of this writing the Home Renovation Savings program, run by Enbridge Gas and Save on Energy, lists foundation and exposed-floor insulation among the upgrades that need a home energy assessment, up to $7,700 across its insulation rebates. Whether your Midland home qualifies is the program’s call. Ask us what is current.
